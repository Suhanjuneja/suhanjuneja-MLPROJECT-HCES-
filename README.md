## What the code actually does

- Reads Level 03 (household characteristics) and Level 14 (A1/B1/C1 questionnaire summary records) directly from the original ZIP.
- Joins on six household identifiers after normalizing inconsistent leading-zero formats.
- Uses **official subtotal item codes** instead of adding item records on top of subtotals.
- Converts 7-day records with `×30/7` and 365-day records with `×30/365`; 30-day values are used directly.
- Excludes imputed house-rent code 539. Forms five shares from the total of all retained summary categories, including an unmodeled residual category.
- Scales five share variables using a StandardScaler fitted to a seeded 45,000-record random subsample.
- Fits K-means and MiniBatch K-means for 2–6 clusters and selects by a same-sample silhouette criterion.
- Compares Ward on a separate **equal-sized 2,100-record benchmark**; checks ARI for repeated subsamples and a feature ablation.
- Produces 12 charts plus anonymized aggregate CSV/JSON outputs. **No row-level data are exported.**

## Interpretations and limitations

**These are exploratory sample-level profiles, not official MPCE estimates.** They do not identify income, socioeconomic status, diet quality, nutritional sufficiency or causal effects. Silhouette 0.22 is weak-to-moderate rather than compelling separation. In particular, the small processed-food cluster has many single-person households, and the 7-day observation period may amplify transitory patterns. Sample rural/urban percentages are **not weighted population estimates**. No official HCES multiplier formulas, standard errors or survey-design variance calculations are applied.

The exact coding reference is the MoSPI HCES 2022–23 official Questionnaire Appendix A: https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/HCES-22-23/AppendixA.pdf. Survey methodology was also supplied as a separate PDF.
